◈Lunar Terrain & Lighting

Working with lunar digital elevation models and albedo-based surface data to improve the fidelity of terrain and lighting in simulation. This supports more realistic visual scenes for navigation testing.

◎Camera & Image Modeling

Using the Brown–Conrady camera distortion model to represent departures from an ideal pinhole camera. The radial coefficients k1 and k2 model how image points shift with radius from the optical center and help evaluate the realism of simulated imagery.

r2 = x2 + y2 xd = x (1 + k1r2 + k2r4) yd = y (1 + k1r2 + k2r4)

Here x and y are normalized undistorted coordinates, while xd and yd are the distorted image coordinates. The full Brown–Conrady model can also include tangential distortion terms.

▦Verification & Analysis

Contributing to scalable testing workflows, including Monte Carlo-style analysis concepts, image-quality checks, and cross-platform debugging in Linux-based simulation environments.

Undergraduate Design II

S.C.R.A.P. was a team-based conceptual active debris removal project aimed at removing high-value orbital debris and defunct satellites from low Earth orbit. The mission combined a contactless electromagnetic de-tumble concept with a passive drag-based de-orbit package called GOLD (Gossamer Orbit Lowering Device).

My work focused on developing a MATLAB model to estimate the descent of the GOLD-equipped target using factors such as drag area, altitude, mass, and related orbital/atmospheric parameters. I also created the risk analysis. As a team, we defined the overall mission architecture, requirements, de-tumble concept, GOLD attachment/de-orbit approach, and supporting subsystem analyses.
